# Example synthesis: changing readings do not identify a mechanism

**Fictional teaching example combining two invented observations.**

The [changing-temperature pilot](/sources/example-temperature-observation.md) shows why it was tempting to describe the sensor difference as drift. The [fixed-temperature follow-up](/sources/example-fixed-temperature-observation.md) challenges the simplest elapsed-time explanation, but changes several conditions at once.

The conclusion within this example is therefore limited: an increasing difference was noted in the first session but not in the follow-up. Neither observation identifies a mechanism or establishes which changed condition mattered. The [analysis](/analyses/example-analysis.md) turns that uncertainty into a proposed discriminating comparison rather than a causal conclusion.

For the [project](/entities/example-project.md), distinguishing plausible explanations is the proposed next research objective. It does not establish novelty, generalize to other sensors, or provide a thesis-level contribution.

This interpretation should be revised if controlled observations distinguish the hypotheses or show that the original pattern is not reproducible.
