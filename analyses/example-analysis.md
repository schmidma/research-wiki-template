# Example analysis: what explains the changing sensor difference?

**Fictional teaching example.**

## What the observations establish

In the [first pilot](/sources/example-temperature-observation.md), time and temperature changed together. In the [follow-up](/sources/example-fixed-temperature-observation.md), temperature was controlled, but warm-up and enclosure conditions also changed.

Taken together, these observations do not identify the cause. They support keeping several explanations open rather than treating [drift](/concepts/example-concept.md) as an observed fact.

## Hypotheses to distinguish

- The difference depends on temperature.
- The difference changes during sensor or reference warm-up.
- A time-dependent change remains under comparable conditions.

These explanations are not necessarily mutually exclusive.

## Proposed next comparison

After a consistent warm-up period, compare fixed-temperature sessions with sessions that vary temperature in a repeated, counterbalanced sequence. Keep placement and acquisition settings consistent and check reference stability independently. Record the difference and temperature throughout each session.

Before collecting data, specify measurement uncertainty and a difference large enough to matter for the intended use. Repeated sessions would help assess reproducibility.

**This is a plan, not a result.** Even a reproducible temperature association would not, by itself, identify which instrument caused it or establish a general correction model. The [synthesis](/syntheses/example-synthesis.md) preserves this boundary.
